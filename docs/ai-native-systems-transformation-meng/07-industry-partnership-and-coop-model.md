# 07 Industry Partnership And Co-op Model

## Partnership Principle

Industry partners are part of the learning architecture. They provide problem contexts, review evidence, support field access, and help validate whether student work is useful.

## Partner Tiers

| Tier | Name | Partner Role |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem Sponsor | Provides project brief, context, limited data or examples, milestone feedback, final review |
| 2 | Co-Educator | Provides Tier 1 support plus mentoring, guest workshops, site access, expert interviews, data/workflow access, project clinics |
| 3 | Residency Host | Provides Tier 2 support plus structured supervision, workplace performance evaluation, and deeper implementation access |

Tier 3 may also provide compensation or sponsorship where available. Compensation or sponsorship is optional and not a condition for Tier 3.

## Deep Challenge Commitment Requirements

Before students commit to a deep challenge at the end of Semester 1, the partner must provide:

- Problem brief.
- Sponsor contact.
- Review schedule.
- Data/access plan.
- Expected value statement.

## Deep Challenge Acceptance Standard

A deep challenge is ready for student commitment only when it meets these minimum quality thresholds:

- Problem brief names the system, pain point, affected users or stakeholders, and why it matters.
- Sponsor contact has decision authority or access to a decision-maker.
- Review schedule includes at least one review per major gate.
- Data/access plan states what can be shared, restrictions, and fallback if data is unavailable.
- Expected value statement names intended operational, technical, learning, or user value.
- Approval owner: studio lead approves academic readiness, and sponsor confirms context/access feasibility.

## Continuous Immersion Model

| Term | Industry Engagement |
|---|---|
| Semester 1 | Problem marketplace, site visits, sponsor clinics, interviews, scoping |
| Semester 2 | Regular sponsor reviews, prototype feedback, user/workflow validation, data access |
| Semester 3 | High-intensity co-op, residency, or equivalent high-contact implementation |
| Semester 4 | Final sponsor board, implementation handoff, portfolio evidence, final competency defense input |

## Co-op Design Principle

The co-op is not detached work experience. It is the field implementation phase of the PBL spine.

Duration and intensity can vary across co-op, residency, or equivalent high-contact implementation formats, but each format must include:

- named supervisor.
- Recurring sponsor reviews.
- field evidence log.
- Agreed deliverables.
- Sponsor evaluation.

Students enter co-op, residency, or equivalent high-contact implementation with:

- Diagnosis.
- Charter.
- Learning contract.
- Prototype direction.
- AI operating discipline.
- Evidence plan.

## Partner Value Proposition

Partners receive:

- Structured diagnosis of transformation opportunities.
- Prototypes or workflow interventions.
- Student teams trained in responsible AI use.
- Documented evidence and implementation recommendations.
- Access to future talent.
- Low-risk exploration of AI-native transformation.

## Program Governance Needs

The program needs a partnership function to manage:

- Challenge intake.
- Partner tiering.
- Data/access expectations.
- Student matching.
- Review schedules.
- Sponsor feedback.
- Co-op quality.
- Project risk escalation.
